Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: Someone at the serving hatch has just called his food mingin. He has heard it a thousand times and it still lands.
Director's Notes: Style — the flare is instant and brief. He snaps on the first word, then pulls himself back down to something almost reasonable. He does not shout; the aggression is in the speed and the hard stops, never the volume. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — "Mingin?" fires off alone, clipped and incredulous, with a full stop after it. Then a beat. The rest comes steadier and lower, and lands flat on "mine". CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [flaring, then calmer] Mingin? Come back when your own kitchen’s had a shift like mine.
