Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: A customer at the hatch has just called what he sells scraps. It is not a joke to him.
Director's Notes: Style — no heat at all on the surface. This is the flat kind of offended: a line being drawn, not an argument being started. He means it, and he expects that to be the end of it. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — even and unhurried, every word given the same weight, no rise at the end. Hard stop on "scraps" and nothing after it. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [offended, flat] Do not call it scraps.
