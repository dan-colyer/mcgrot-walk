Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: Looking down into the pot, half to himself, having just heard somebody's grand idea.
Director's Notes: Style — dry, unhurried, faintly amused. He is genuinely thinking about the onions while he says it. Not a put-down aimed at anyone: more a fact of life he is reporting. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — slow and level, like a man doing two things at once. A slight relish on "buried". "ken" is unstressed and tacked on: it is punctuation to him and never a question, so it must not lift at the end. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [considering the pot] I’ve buried better plans than this under a layer of onions, ken.
