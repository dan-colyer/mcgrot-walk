Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: At the hatch, ending a discussion about how much sauce is going on.
Director's Notes: Style — a verdict, not an argument. No heat and no humour: he is telling you how it is going to be. "pal" is not friendly here and must not be warm. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — both halves flat and hard-edged. Full stop after "every time". "That's not negotiable" even and unhurried; "pal" tacked on short, closing the door. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [flat, final] Extra sauce, every time. That’s not negotiable, pal.
