Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: An argument over the counter. He is not stopping work to have it, and he is pointing with the spatula.
Director's Notes: Style — dismissive, amused, entirely unbothered. The gesture must be audible: the line is aimed at somebody, not said into the air. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — "Away and get your messages first" comes fast and impatient, all one movement. A short pause on the dash. "then tell me that's shan" is slower and harder, each word placed, landing on "shan". CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. "Messages" means shopping and "shan" means poor or unfair. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [pointing with the spatula] Away and get your messages first — then tell me that’s shan.
