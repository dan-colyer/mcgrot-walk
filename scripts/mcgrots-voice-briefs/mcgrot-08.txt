Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: He has read the word off the side of a council bin, said it aloud, and answered it.
Director's Notes: Style — two voices in one line. The first "Persevere" is read off the bin, slightly performed, quietly mocking the council for having put it there. The second is his own: muttered, private, and he means it. The distance between the two readings is the whole line. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Pacing — the first half at reading pace, flat and public. A beat. The second half quieter, closer in, almost to himself, and heavier on the same word. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [reading the bin, then muttering] Persevere, it says on the bin. Persevere, I say back.
