Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: A new customer at the hatch, mouthing off a bit. Greeting and warning shot in the same breath.
Director's Notes: Style — begins as a genuine greeting, drops a warning in the middle, and comes out the far side almost friendly. He has said this exact line ten thousand times and it still amuses him slightly. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — "Awright pal" quick and warm. Then "Sauce is extra" flatter and harder. "opinions are free" is slower, with the ghost of a smile in it. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [warning, then softening] Awright pal. Sauce is extra, opinions are free.
