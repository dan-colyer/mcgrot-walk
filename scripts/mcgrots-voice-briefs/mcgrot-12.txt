Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: He is already moving as he says it, heading off down the Walk and expecting you to follow.
Director's Notes: Style — the most open he gets. Proud, energetic, an invitation rather than a challenge, and slightly out of breath because he is walking while he talks. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — quick and forward-leaning from the first word, with no pause anywhere in it. Builds through "a queue that means something", lands hard on "something", then stops dead. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. "Chum me doon" means walk with me and "fit o' the Walk" is a place. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [proud, already walking] Chum me doon the fit o’ the Walk and I’ll show ye a queue that means something.
