Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: He leans in over the counter to say this. Pomplé, his dog, is at his feet, and he glances down at her partway through. This is the closest he gets to tenderness.
Director's Notes: Style — THIS IS THE SINCERE LINE AND THE WHOLE SET IS JUDGED AGAINST IT. He drops in volume, slows down, and the rasp eases — that softening is the point and must be clearly audible against every other line. Not sentimental and not soft: quieter and warmer, said to one person only. The joke is real and so is the affection for the dog. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. The accent does not thin out when he softens; only the volume and the grit do. Pacing — low and unhurried throughout. A small break before "and I believe the dog", and that clause is the gentlest thing in the whole set. No hard stop at the end: it settles rather than lands. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [confidential, glancing down] Pomplé says the sauce needs mercy, and I believe the dog.
