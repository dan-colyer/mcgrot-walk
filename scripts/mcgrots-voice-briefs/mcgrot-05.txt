Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: Leaning on the hatch late on, talking about his own trade to nobody in particular.
Director's Notes: Style — proud, but far too tired to sell it. He is stating a fact, not making a boast, and the pride is real and worn smooth by repetition. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — rolls out steadily, one clause into the next, no push anywhere. A slight lift on "longest" and then straight back down. "fit o' the Walk" is thrown away: it is simply where he works. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. "Steamboats" means drunk and "fit o' the Walk" is a place. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [with weary pride] Steamboats or stone sober, my queue’s still the longest at the fit o’ the Walk.
