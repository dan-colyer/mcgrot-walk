Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: Two customers are being daft in the queue. He does not look up from the pot he is stirring.
Director's Notes: Style — contempt with real affection underneath it. He is stirring while he says it and it costs him no effort whatsoever. Barely raised, never sharp. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Thickest on dialect words, a shade clearer on ordinary sentences. Pacing — "Radge, the pair of you" quick and thrown out sideways. A short pause on the dash while he keeps stirring. "queueing for a smell" drier and slower, the punchline delivered without any interest in whether it lands. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. "Radge" means mad or wild. Read every word exactly as printed; never anglicise, correct, or explain it.

McGrot: [unimpressed, stirring] Radge, the pair of you — queueing for a smell.
