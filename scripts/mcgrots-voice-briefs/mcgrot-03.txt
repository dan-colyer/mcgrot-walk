Audio Profile: McGrot is a Leith street-food vendor in his early fifties, thickset and stooped, proprietor of the Gullet — a van at the foot of Leith Walk. Mid-range voice, not deep and not booming, worn to dry gravel by cigarettes, hot oil, and years of cold salt wind off the water. Young enough to still be physically capable: the damage is earned by hard use, not by age.
Scene: He has been asked for something at the hatch and the answer was never going to be yes.
Director's Notes: Style — one syllable, complete in itself. Not curt, not sarcastic, not wounded: a whole sentence that happens to be one word. THIS IS THE HARDEST LINE IN THE SET. It must not sound like the opening of a longer sentence he was cut off in, and it must not sound like a grunt. It is an answer, and it is final. Accent — thick Leith, Edinburgh dockside. Glottal stops, hard consonants, phlegmy. Not English, not Irish, not generic television Scottish. Pacing — immediate, with no intake of breath before it and nothing after. Flat pitch, no lift at the end. Let the silence that follows carry the rest. CRITICAL: the dialect is not a mistake and is not an accent being performed. Read the word exactly as printed; never anglicise it to "no".

McGrot: [flat, immediate] Naw.
