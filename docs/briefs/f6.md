# F6 — the camera glides between anchors, it does not jump

Dan's call, 2026-08-12, in his words: *"let's just try and make any camera cuts
smooth."* That settles the question the phase gate raised in `.herdr/gate.md`
§ F6, and it settles it with an option the gate did not offer. Do not implement
"cut on departure" or "cut on arrival". Both are cuts. He does not want a cut.

## The fault

`src/mcgrots/main.js:183` — `goTo()` assigns `current = a` before the walk
begins. `frame()` then calls `placeCamera()` every tick from `current.camera`.
So the camera is in the destination shot on frame 1 while the actor is still at
the source.

Measured by the gate on one `goTo('far')` from `counter`:

```
camera moved on frame 1:  10.324 m
actor  moved on frame 1:   0.020 m
```

Reproduce that before you change anything. A fix you cannot see fail first is a
fix you cannot prove.

## What to build

The camera travels from the source anchor's pose to the destination's over the
course of the walk, and arrives when the actor arrives.

Four constraints, all load-bearing:

1. **Tie the interpolation to the actor's walk progress, not to a duration of
   your own.** The actor already knows where it started, where it is going and
   whether it is still walking. A second timer will drift out of step with it
   and the camera will land early or late. If the actor does not currently
   expose progress, adding a read-only accessor is in scope; a duplicate
   copy of its pathing is not.

2. **A snap stays instant.** `goTo(id, { snap: true })` is a panel change by
   definition — the boot call and S4's gutter cut both depend on it. Only the
   walk branch eases. This is your control: the snap path must measure exactly
   as it does today.

3. **The parked pose must not move by a pixel.** When the actor is at rest at
   an anchor the camera is where it is now. The eased path is what changes,
   not either endpoint. If any existing check moves, you have changed a
   resting shot and that is a regression, not a recapture. Do not recapture.

4. **Deterministic under frame-stepping.** `mcgrots-shot.mjs` and the gate
   freeze rAF and step every frame by hand. Drive the easing from the `dt`
   already passed into `frame()` — never `performance.now()`, never a wall
   clock. Two runs of the same command must give the same picture.

Easing curve is yours. Something with soft ends will read better than linear;
say what you chose and why in the commit.

## The gate

One new check in the `anchors` region (or wherever the existing anchor checks
live — do not invent a region for one check):

- **The claim:** on a walk, the camera's frame-1 movement is a small fraction
  of the total distance it will travel.
- **The control:** the same measurement on the snap path, which must stay at
  effectively the whole distance on frame 1. On-vs-off through one code path is
  not isolation; walk-vs-snap is, because the two branches genuinely differ.
- **The fault injection:** restore `current = a` ahead of the walk. The walk
  arm must go red and the snap arm must stay green. Commit before you inject —
  `git checkout <paths>` restores nothing if any path in the list is untracked.

Assert on the *arrival* too, or the gate passes for a camera that never gets
there: when the actor stops, the camera pose equals the destination anchor's
within a tight epsilon.
