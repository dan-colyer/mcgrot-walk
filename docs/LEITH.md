# The Leith Bible

Reference dossier for making the simulation more genuinely Leith. Compiled
2026-08-01 from a research pass over documented Leith history, oral-history
projects, and local journalism; sources inline. E4 briefs draw the cast and
dynamics from here. Dialogue lines are **invented in the spirit of documented
Leith speech** unless marked otherwise — treat them as register samples, not
scripts.

## The test for every joke

**Would it get a laugh in the Central Bar, said aloud, with Leithers present?**
Mockery of Leith fails. Mockery of Edinburgh, developers, tram works and
nervous students passes. When in doubt, aim the joke at a body with a headed
letterhead. And Scots is the *prestige* register in this game — the language
of wit and authority, never a signal of low status; the RP-speaking students
are the ones linguistically out of their depth.

## Cast — fifteen Leither archetypes

1. **The Foot-of-the-Walk Auld Boy.** Retired docker's son, 70s, flat cap,
   daily slow circuit past the Queen Victoria statue (Leith's meeting point
   since 1907). Watches roadworks with hands behind his back. ~1,000 dockers
   were on the books at Leith Docks in the late 1940s (Living Memory
   Association); his faither was one. *"Ma faither shifted four million ton o'
   cargo through they docks. You cannae shift a bin."* On the comics: listens
   fully, nods once. "Heard worse in the Dockers Club."
2. **The Queen of Leith.** Pub landlady modelled on the real Mary Moriarty of
   the Port O' Leith (25 years behind the bar; served judges, sailors, working
   girls and labourers side by side). Doorway sentry; knows everyone's name,
   business and mother. *"Sit doon afore ye fa' doon."* On the comics:
   delighted; heckles constructively; requests specific issues.
3. **The Hibee.** Green-and-white scarf in all seasons; Easter Road is ten
   minutes east. Sings "Sunshine on Leith" quietly to himself, even now. On
   the comics: only interested if a Hibs reference got garbled in — then
   transfixed.
4. **The Persevere Pensioner.** Her grandmother voted in the 1920 plebiscite —
   29,891 against amalgamation with Edinburgh, 5,357 for; Edinburgh did it
   anyway. Treats this as recent news. *"This isnae Edinburgh, son. This is
   Leith. We voted. They didnae listen. 1920. Persevere."*
5. **The Save Leith Walk Veteran.** Tote bag, laminated petition; veteran of
   the real campaign that stopped the demolition of the red sandstone parade
   at Stead's Place (home of Leith Depot). Still campaigning — now to save the
   rubble. On the comics: community arts provision; fully supportive.
6. **The Tram Works Shop Owner.** The street's Cassandra. Leith endured the
   2000s utility works, lost the line entirely in 2011, then got three more
   years of digging from 2019. *"Three years o' barriers ootside ma shop. Then
   the world ended. Honestly? Footfall's aboot the same."*
7. **The Polish Deli Owner.** Second generation; code-switches Polish → broad
   Leith Scots mid-sentence (the Walk has multiple Polish delis). *"Kiełbasa's
   finished, pierogi's finished. Awright pal, whit can I get ye — we've got
   tins."* Compares the comics unfavourably to Polish comics: also
   incomprehensible, better drawn.
8. **The Valvona & Crolla Matriarch.** Scotland's oldest deli, Elm Row.
   Guards the last wheel of parmesan in Scotland. Listens to readings with
   operatic emotional investment; weeps at garbled romance panels.
9. **The Banana Flats Sentinel.** Teenager on a bike doing loops from Cables
   Wynd House (A-listed 2017; Sick Boy's building). Fierce pride. *"Aye it's a
   listed buildin', ken. Whit's your gaff listed as?"* Pretends not to care
   about the comics; circles back for the ending.
10. **The Posh Students.** Three of them, doon the Walk for the first time —
    Edinburgh students really were warned Leith was "dodgy", warnings locals
    now find laughable. One treats it as a Trainspotting pilgrimage. *"Do we
    say 'ken'? Rupert, do NOT say 'ken'."* They film the readings; one calls
    it "actually quite a powerful piece". **Arc: Rupert ends up genuinely
    loving the Walk — won over, not humiliated.**
11. **The New-Build Gentrifier.** Waterfront flat; apologetic; over-performs
    localness ("Leithers Don't Litter" tote, says "the Walk" one beat too
    deliberately). Asks if the vendor does a newsletter.
12. **The Central Bar Regular.** Anchored to the tiled Victorian "people's
    palace" at the Foot. He has a seat; it is his seat. *"This pub survived
    the station gettin' demolished, the trams, an' noo this. Same again."*
13. **The Gala Day Organiser.** Leith Festival's pageant has processed doon
    the Walk to the Links since 1907, through two world wars. Clipboard;
    salvaged bunting. *"You — you're a float now. Congratulations."*
14. **The Street Drinker Philosopher.** Handle with care (see Sensitivity).
    Sharpest wit on the street; holds court on a bench; the vendor's most
    loyal audience, offers editorial notes. *"End o' the world? Pal, they said
    that aboot the amalgamation."* Works only if players leave thinking he is
    the wisest NPC.
15. **The Oral History Lady.** Modelled on the Living Memory Association
    (recording Leithers since 1986). Cassette recorder; interviewing
    survivors. *"Nae detail's too wee."* Records every reading: "That's
    heritage now, that."

## Texture — "aye, that's the Walk right enough"

1. The **Queen Victoria statue** at the Foot — THE meeting point since 1907;
   a 1985 restoration found a glass casket of 1907 Leith newspapers inside.
2. **"PERSEVERE"** everywhere — buildings, bins, tattoos; the ship crest.
3. The **Central Bar's tiled interior** — floor-to-ceiling Victorian tiles,
   sporting scenes, stained glass. Even ruined, the tiles survive.
4. The big Tesco that was **Leith Central Station** — whose derelict hangar
   gave Trainspotting its title scene.
5. The **red sandstone parade at Stead's Place** — Save Leith Walk banners.
6. The **Banana Flats' curve** on the skyline (Cables Wynd House, 1965).
7. **Tram infrastructure, half-finished forever** — the premise is barely
   fiction; the first passenger tram down the Walk (June 2023) came 67 years
   after the last one. In-game, that day never came.
8. The **polyglot shop strip** — Polish deli, Chinese supermarket, Italian
   delis, 24-hour bakeries, ancient pubs in one unbroken run.
9. **Valvona & Crolla** at Elm Row — hanging salamis, wine crates.
10. **Gulls** — entitled, enormous, the apex predator of this apocalypse.
11. **Green and white on match day** — scarves in windows, the tidal flow
    east to Easter Road; the Harp & Castle as the Hibs pub.
12. **Gala Day bunting** — second Saturday in June, floats, "best dressed".
13. **Leith Theatre** — gifted as amalgamation compensation, bombed, revived.
14. **"Leithers Don't Litter" / boundary plaque** where Edinburgh ends.

## Social dynamics worth simulating

- **Leith v Edinburgh (the 1920 grudge).** The deepest. Pride, not
  bitterness; the comedy is that the apocalypse changes nothing. The one
  thing uniting every archetype — auld boy, student, gentrifier — is that
  Leith is not Edinburgh.
- **Old Leith v New Leith.** Target the *anxiety of the incomer*, never the
  poverty of the local; locals always hold higher status in the scene. The
  redemptive beat is real: Save Leith Walk united artists AND lifelong
  residents against a developer.
- **The tram works, eternal.** Grumbling is a bonding ritual, a
  weather-substitute. NPCs cannot distinguish bomb damage from tram works:
  *"Was that the war?" "Naw, that's been like that since 2019."*
- **Match day.** The whole crowd sim flows east; the vendors lose their
  entire audience for 90 minutes. Hibs fandom is hope as a discipline —
  "persevere" in football form. Hearts rivalry stays gentle slagging.
- **The world's high street.** Port-town cosmopolitanism predates
  gentrification by a century; the comedy is everyone being Leith *first* —
  the Polish deli owner out-Scotting the Scots.

## Sensitivity — where the line is

- **Addiction / Trainspotting:** reference the mythology (locations, the
  title joke, the pilgrimage absurdity), never the pathology. No needle
  imagery, no junkie NPCs, no HIV-era references. If a character has
  troubles, give them the best lines and never make the trouble the joke.
- **Poverty:** the Banana Flats are celebrated — A-listed, defended by their
  residents, and in 1965 they were the *escape from* slum housing. Punching
  down = squalor gags, scheme stereotypes. Celebrating = the building as a
  landmark equal to the castle.
- **Students/gentrifiers:** the safe target (up or sideways), but let them be
  won over, not humiliated.
- **Orange walks / sectarianism: leave out entirely.** Match-day joy yes;
  sectarian texture has no comedic upside and high harm.
- **Institutions, not people:** the 1920 grudge and tram anger are safe
  because they punch at bodies with letterheads.
